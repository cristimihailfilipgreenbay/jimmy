# Membrane transport

## Passive transport

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. It needs no energy from the cell.

Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Water moves towards the side with the higher solute concentration. In a hypotonic solution a cell gains water and may burst; in a hypertonic solution it loses water and shrinks.

Facilitated diffusion uses channel proteins and carrier proteins to move molecules such as glucose and ions down their concentration gradient. It is still passive, but it is specific and can saturate.

## Active transport

Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient and needs energy, usually ATP.

The sodium-potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase) moves three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions in for each ATP used. This keeps the resting membrane potential and drives secondary transport of glucose.

## Bulk transport

Endocytosis brings large particles into the cell by folding the membrane around them. Exocytosis releases material by fusing vesicles with the membrane.
